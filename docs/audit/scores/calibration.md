## What the gaps say

- **Legibility (gap 2–3).** Claude read "type clears the floor". The owner reads
  "would I read this on a phone" — and the answer is no: body copy is small
  against the headline, the screenshots are unreadable at feed scale, the panels
  are dense. Anchor rewritten: a 4 needs the BODY to read at 393px without
  effort, not just the headline.
- **Hierarchy (gap 2 on both).** Claude scored the stack (eyebrow → headline →
  rule → body) as a hierarchy. The owner sees the same stack on every slide as
  no hierarchy at all — nothing is ever the ONE thing. Anchor rewritten: a 3
  needs at least one element per slide that dominates the frame.
- **CTA (gap 3 on aftercare).** A gold button with the right keyword is not
  enough; the owner scored the close a 1. Reading the reference list (WHOOP's
  one-metric card, Headspace's six words on a slide), the close must be an
  arrival, not a form: one line, one button, nothing else.
- **Fidelity and specificity (gap 2 on aftercare).** Claude checked facts
  against the brief; the owner judged whether the deck TEACHES what the post
  teaches. Four bare rows with no notes ("What was done", "A name at the end")
  are faithful and empty. Anchor rewritten: rows without the reason behind them
  score as filler.
- **Variety** agrees at 2. **Brand** agrees within 1.

## The bar, from the reference list

The owner's twenty accounts share one rule: the visual is CONSTRUCTED — a
chart, a diagram, an illustration, a cropped interface at real scale, a
typographic system — and carries information. Photography only when it pulls
the reader into reading. Named steals to design toward: one metric blown up
with a second-person sentence (WHOOP); a fixed skeleton readers learn once
(Axios); a single type lockup that survives any image (Dezeen); crop the UI to
the one element that changed, at real scale (Notion); a whole slide on six
words is a legitimate beat (Headspace); a fixed brand element in the same place
on every post (The Economist's red rule).

## Rule for Claude's scoring from here

Score as the owner would: the phone in one hand, one second per slide. If a
slide has to be read to be understood, legibility and hierarchy cap at 2.
